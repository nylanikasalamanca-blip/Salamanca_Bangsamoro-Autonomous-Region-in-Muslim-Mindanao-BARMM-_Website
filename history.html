Our History & Culture

A legacy of royal sultanates, faith, freedom, and timeless craftsmanship

A Brief History of Bangsamoro

The history of the Bangsamoro region is deeply tied to the introduction of Islam in the late 14th century by Islamic missionary Sheikh Karimul Makhdum. Following his arrival, powerful sovereign states were founded, including the Sultanate of Sulu and the Sultanate of Maguindanao under Sultan Kudarat. These sultanates developed sophisticated legal, diplomatic, and trade networks across Southeast Asia long before foreign colonizers arrived in the archipelago.


Throughout centuries of Spanish and American colonial rule, the Bangsamoro people fiercely maintained their independence, identity, and cultural heritage. Their historic resilience eventually culminated in the creation of the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) in 2019, inaugurating a modern era of peace, governance, and cultural preservation.

Cultural Highlights

Famous Festivals

Shariff Kabungsuwan Festival

Celebrated in Cotabato City with colorful river parades commemorating the arrival of Islam in mainland Mindanao.

Kamahardikaan Festival

Tawi-Tawi's founding anniversary celebrating Sama culture, marine heritage, and traditional dances.

Meguyaya Festival

A harvest festival in Upi celebrating harmony among indigenous, Muslim, and Christian communities.

Traditional Crafts & Heritage

Inaul Fabric

Royal handwoven silk and cotton textiles featuring vivid color combinations and intricate geometric patterns.

Okir Woodcarving

Traditional artistic motif characterized by elegant flowing plant vines and scrollwork found in Torogan royal houses.

Kulintang Music

Traditional gong-chime music ensemble performed during royal ceremonies and celebrations.
